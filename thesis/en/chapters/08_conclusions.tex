\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:concl-summary}

This thesis set out to break the circularity that undermines data-driven transit
planning and to build, in its place, a demand-driven pipeline that diagnoses unmet need,
generates candidate corridors, and transfers to other cities. The four research
questions can now be answered directly.

\paragraph{RQ1 -- Demand.} \emph{Yes.} Transit demand can be estimated at \ageb
resolution from Tier-1 data alone. The trip-generation and doubly-constrained gravity
model of \cref{sec:w1} produce a metropolitan demand surface using only census, economic,
and network variables, with no transit input, and calibration against the EOD~2022 survey
(\cref{sec:w2}) fixes the distance-decay parameter at $\betadecay=1.2005$---a value that
fits the observed flows better than the conventional prior. Demand is therefore never a
function of current supply, which is the precondition the rest of the framework depends on.

\paragraph{RQ2 -- Diagnosis.} \emph{Yes, and it validates out of sample.} The
coverage-gap index (\cref{sec:w3}) sets modeled demand against \textsc{gtfs}-measured
accessibility, yielding a dependent variable constructed independently of supply; the
supervised retrain learns it from place characteristics with no leakage
(\cref{tab:retrain}). Its strongest support is the Line~4 natural experiment: a corridor
the model never saw, later chosen for construction, shows \num{1.6} times the
metropolitan High-gap rate. This diagnostic is the thesis's firmest and most citable
contribution.

\paragraph{RQ3 -- Generation.} \emph{Partially, and now characterized.} Once corridors
are shaped as real paths (a minimum-spanning-tree diameter trunk on the road graph) and
judged by anchor-directness rather than endpoint detour (\cref{sec:w6}), the generator
produces at least one substantive, feasible, merit-passing corridor---\code{W6\_G02},
\SI{12.1}{\kilo\metre}, \SI{56}{\percent} High-gap, best served-\ageb Jaccard
non-redundancy \num{0.18} (though a shape-overlap measure with an existing premium
route is a less clean \SI{42}{\percent}, \cref{sec:res-w6}), \num{73}rd-percentile
demand efficiency. The anchor-directness substitution for endpoint-detour is not
narrowly tuned to this one corridor: reported side by side for the full candidate set
(\cref{tab:directness-comparison}), it rescues three of four feasible \zmg corridors
from what would otherwise be near-uniform rejection under endpoint-detour. The
generator also surfaces lower-efficiency connectors alongside \code{W6\_G02}, and it
does not reconstruct built rail lines---an expected outcome, since reconstruction is a
weak proxy for merit (\cref{sec:disc-reconstruction}). One further qualification is
necessary and is not swept into a footnote: the merit tests and the framework's own W5
Pareto ranking, as originally defined, disagreed on which corridor is best---\code{W6\_G03},
not \code{W6\_G02}, was the sole Pareto-non-dominated feasible corridor under every tested
objective weighting (\cref{sec:res-w6,sec:disc-generative}). Diagnosing why resolved the
disagreement rather than merely disclosing it: $f_1$'s rate formulation mechanically
favored small, homogeneous candidates, and an absolute-coverage companion
($f_1^{\text{tot}}$, \cref{eq:f1-total}) recovers a genuine four-way Pareto front in which
\emph{none} of the four feasible corridors dominates the others. The real choice the
generator presents is therefore a scale-versus-targeting-purity trade-off---fund fewer,
larger corridors addressing more absolute unmet demand (\code{W6\_G02}, \code{W6\_G01}) or
a smaller, highly-targeted connector (\code{W6\_G03})---not an unresolved internal
contradiction. The generative layer is thus a genuine but bounded contribution whose
internal evaluation criteria are now reconciled, and its headline corridor,
\code{W6\_G02}, should still be read as a hypothesis for a feasibility study, not as a
specification of mode, alignment, or cost (\cref{sec:concl-recommendations}).

\paragraph{RQ4 -- Transferability.} \emph{Yes, within Mexico's national data ecosystem,
end-to-end.} The full W1--W8 pipeline runs on two contrasting metropolitan
areas---Toluca (large, sixteen municipalities) and Aguascalientes (compact,
three)---re-deriving demand, diagnosis, weights, corridors, and audit from each
city's own Tier-1 and \textsc{gtfs} data (\cref{ch:transferability}). The diagnostic
differentiates the three metros sensibly by unmet-demand share, each city's
weighting re-derives from its own data rather than inheriting \zmg's, and the
demand/equity alignment finding of \cref{sec:disc-equity} replicates in both
transfer cities (\cref{tab:equity-transfer}). ``Without bespoke re-engineering''
requires one qualification: several fixed constants (the \num{1.8} anchor-directness
cap, the \SI{300}{\metre} stop-spacing floor, demand thresholds) are carried over
unchanged rather than re-derived, and one of them---the stop-spacing floor---is the
direct, demonstrated cause of Toluca's W7 audit returning zero feasible routes
against its \SI{43}{\metre}-median-spacing network. This is read here as an artifact
of that specific constant rather than of the diagnostic or generative logic, but it
is a real, not merely theoretical, limit on ``no re-engineering,'' and the two
transfer cities share \zmg's national data infrastructure rather than testing a
different country's.

\section{Practical recommendations}
\label{sec:concl-recommendations}

For a planning agency, the framework is most useful as a \emph{diagnostic screen} rather
than an autopilot. Three recommendations follow. First, treat the coverage-gap surface
as the primary product: it ranks where unmet demand is concentrated at a resolution finer
than any single line, and it can be recomputed cheaply as census and \textsc{gtfs} data
are updated. Second, use the route audit (\cref{sec:w7}) to flag low-demand, indirect,
and redundant existing routes for review---in the transfer cities it surfaced substantial
parallel-service redundancy that is a candidate for rationalization---though in
concessioned systems like Toluca's, where roughly thirty independent operators run the
flagged routes, ``review'' in practice means a multi-year negotiation and
formalization process, not an internal scheduling exercise, and the audit's practical
value there is in quantifying the scale of the parallelism problem for a negotiating
agency rather than in directly specifying which routes to cut. Third, and repeated
here deliberately rather than confined to this paragraph alone, read \emph{every}
generated corridor---including \code{W6\_G02}---as a \emph{hypothesis to be studied},
not as a final alignment: the multi-objective function has no capital-cost or
right-of-way term, so a merit-passing corridor is at most a defensible starting point
for a feasibility study that must independently establish mode, alignment, and cost;
it is not itself that feasibility study, and the generator's characterized
limitations mean its output should inform, not replace, engineering and community
process.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:concl-future}

Four directions would most strengthen the framework. First, \emph{local calibration for
the transfer cities}: Toluca and Aguascalientes currently inherit \zmg's distance-decay
prior, and a survey-calibrated $\betadecay$ per city would tighten their demand surfaces.
Second, a \emph{network-distance gravity model}: replacing Euclidean impedance with
travel-time impedance on the road graph would improve absolute demand magnitudes. Third,
\emph{transferring the supervised layer}: the coverage-gap retrain was run only for \zmg,
and applying it to the transfer cities would test whether the learned relationship between
place characteristics and unmet demand is itself portable. Fourth, a \emph{hybrid corridor
shaper} that switches between diameter-trunk and travelling-salesman routing where the
latter remains feasible. Exploratory testing quantifies rather than merely asserts this:
for \code{W6\_G00} a full-terminal TSP path is a small, still-feasible win (+2 AGEBs, +9,500
trips/day, anchor-directness 1.76 versus the 1.8 cap); for \code{W6\_G01}, the group with
the largest gap between the two shapers, TSP would capture roughly twice the demand
(+12 AGEBs, +89,000 trips/day) but pushes the route to 31.8 km, past the 30 km cap, making
it infeasible. The diameter trunk's dropped demand is therefore the price of feasibility
for the larger candidates, not an unexamined shortcoming, and a hybrid shaper's expected
gain is bounded by the one small win it would recover (\code{W6\_G00}) against the one
larger gain it would forfeit to infeasibility (\code{W6\_G01}) under the current length
cap. Beyond these, the clearest external validation
would come from observed ridership on any corridor that is eventually built along a
generated alignment---the counterfactual that, by construction, no purely ex-ante study
can supply.
